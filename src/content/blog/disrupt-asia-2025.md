## A Milestone for MedTech in Sri Lanka

The air at **TRACE Expert City** was vibrating with a specific kind of energy last Friday—the hum of drones, the buzz of conversation, and the palpable excitement of the future being built in real-time. This was **Disrupt Asia 2025**, Sri Lanka's premier startup innovation festival, and for us at **ExoGenic**, it was our coming-out party.

For months, our team has been working quietly in the lab, refining algorithms and testing servo motors. But technology doesn't exist in a vacuum. Disrupt Asia was our chance to step into the light and answer the question: *Can a Sri Lankan startup build world-class bionic limbs?*

---

### Unveiling Project Dexios

Our booth was small, but the crowd involved was anything but. We showcased **Project Dexios**, our flagship MyoElectric Prosthetic Device.

What surprised us most wasn't the technical questions about EMG signal processing or battery life (though there were plenty of those). It was the look on people's faces when they saw the arm move. When a student volunteer strapped on the sensor cuff and watched the robotic fingers mirror his own, the gap between "science fiction" and "reality" vanished.

---

### Beyond the Tech

While the technical validation was encouraging, the real value of Disrupt Asia lay in the human connections.

*   **The Ecosystem:** We met fellow founders facing the same supply chain hurdles and regulatory mazes. There's a shared camaraderie in the Sri Lankan startup ecosystem that is incredibly empowering.
*   **The Mentors:** Industry heavyweights stopped by to offer not just praise, but hard questions. *How do you scale? What is your clinical trial pathway?* These are the questions that sharpen a startup's vision.
*   **The Future:** We had promising conversations with potential investors who understand that MedTech is a marathon, not a sprint.

---

### Moving Forward

We left the festival exhausted but electrified. The validation we received at Disrupt Asia is fuel for the long nights ahead. We aren't just building a prosthetic arm; we are building a case for what Sri Lankan engineering can achieve.

To everyone who stopped by, asked questions, or simply gave us a thumbs up: **Thank you.** You are part of our journey now.
