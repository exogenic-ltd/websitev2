## A Milestone for MedTech in Sri Lanka

Last Friday at TRACE Expert City, the atmosphere carried a particular intensity—the mechanical hum of demonstration drones, the animated discussions between founders and investors, the unmistakable energy of innovation materializing before our eyes. Disrupt Asia 2025, Sri Lanka's foremost startup innovation festival, represented a pivotal moment for ExoGenic: our first major public debut.

For months, our team had worked in relative obscurity, iterating on control algorithms and calibrating servo motors. Yet technology, however sophisticated, cannot develop in isolation. Disrupt Asia offered us the opportunity to confront a fundamental question: can a Sri Lankan startup compete in the global market for advanced prosthetic technology?

---

### Unveiling Project Dexios

![ExoGenic Booth at Disrupt Asia](/assets/images/blog/disrupt-booth.jpg)

Our exhibition space was modest, but the engagement it generated was anything but. We presented Project Dexios, our flagship myoelectric prosthetic device, to an audience that ranged from curious students to seasoned medical professionals.

The most striking moments came not from the inevitable technical inquiries about EMG signal processing or power management—though these were numerous and welcome—but from the visceral reactions when observers watched the device respond in real-time. When a volunteer donned the sensor array and witnessed the prosthetic fingers replicate their own movements, the boundary between speculative fiction and practical engineering dissolved entirely.

---

### Beyond the Technical Demonstration

While the technical validation proved encouraging, the festival's true value lay in the relationships it fostered.

*   **The Ecosystem:** We encountered fellow entrepreneurs navigating identical challenges—supply chain constraints, regulatory complexity, manufacturing partnerships. The solidarity within Sri Lanka's startup community, particularly among those pursuing hardware innovation, proved both reassuring and energizing.
*   **The Mentors:** Several industry veterans took time to engage substantively with our work, posing the difficult questions that matter: How do you plan to scale manufacturing? What regulatory pathway will you pursue for clinical trials? These conversations, however challenging, refined our strategic thinking considerably.
*   **The Capital:** We initiated discussions with investors who understand that medical technology development operates on longer timescales than software ventures, requiring patience and sustained commitment.

---

### Moving Forward

We departed the festival physically exhausted but intellectually invigorated. The reception Project Dexios received has reinforced our conviction that we are pursuing work of genuine consequence. This is not merely about building a prosthetic device; it is about demonstrating what Sri Lankan engineering talent can accomplish when given the opportunity.

To everyone who visited our booth, engaged with our work, or offered encouragement: we are grateful. You have become part of this endeavor.
